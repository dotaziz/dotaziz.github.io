Today, December 11, 2025, I've decided to self-learn most of the computer science concepts I haven't had the opportunity to learn. Although I'm currently pursuing a BSc in IT at the University of Cape Coast, I largely feel my education limits me to an extent.

I've been programming professionally since 2022, and I haven't had the opportunity to learn the most pressing computer science concepts that would make me a better software engineer. My current degree doesn't really cover those parts either. In due time, I'll talk about my experience at UCC as a student, the disappointments and all.

I want to become a competent software engineer and be better with my craftsmanship. Two things: (1) keep building software without ceasing, (2) focus more on the philosophical aspects of software engineering and programming. I know I've got to build and keep building, but to what extent?

I learned programming using YouTube videos and mostly books, so I don't really have a solid grasp on the key things and skills needed to build software that transforms lives and empowers other software developers.

I read articles from danluu.com, Paul Graham, Hacker News (posts and comments). I always ask myself: how can I be as competent and highly skilled as these individuals? Perhaps I need to spend more time coding, or spend more time reading their posts and discussions, or maybe I can never reach that pinnacle of competence.

After all, I am just a random individual from Ghana, what can I do and what do I know?

But I've also been thinking perhaps the perfect language or framework would do it. Yet I know quite well that languages and frameworks are just tools to help one achieve an end. I truly know that learning another language or framework wouldn't make me a highly competent craftsman. The irony is I keep flirting with these ideas every day as though I am an outlier.

I've been programming in JavaScript for about 5 years now, and for these past years I've been thinking about how to better my craft, yet still I do nothing about it. I've learned Go thinking it would make me a better programmer. At one point in time, I even thought of learning Zig or Rust. I read from countless forums that it makes one a better craftsman and changes how you think. I read at one time that to be a better programmer, we need to first master the tools we use every day (from Gergely Orosz, or someone similar).

I know all these, yet why do I always feel as though learning another language apart from JavaScript would make me a better programmer?

I've come to a conclusion that perhaps I don't have a solid foundation in all the major related concepts in computer science or even software engineering (i.e., programming, databases, networking, operating systems, data structures and algorithms). And as a way to buffer or compensate for my lack of understanding of these, I would tell myself that, "Oh, learning language/tool X would make me a better developer."

That's the conflict I've been in for the past 3-ish years. Maybe it's just me overthinking, or maybe not. But what I am sure of is that I am not a good software craftsman...and I've been coding all this while.

I've decided to face the reality. I am taking all the recommended areas in https://teachyourselfcs.com.

I promise and pledge to take this roadmap wholly and wholeheartedly.

I am starting with SICP. I'll be updating this blog with progress reports.
