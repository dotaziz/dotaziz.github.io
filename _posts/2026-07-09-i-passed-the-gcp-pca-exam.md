**Phew!** 

After an intense 2 hours of sitting in my hostel room behind my laptop with racing heart and sweaty palms, I was stuck...confused about whether I had passed the exam or not. I didn't fully read or comprehend the message I got after the 120-minute mark. I just closed the OnVUE software, asking myself whether I had passed or not.

The preparation towards the PCA exam wasn't a small feat. I didn't fully comprehend most GCP concepts going in. I've been working with GCP for about 2 years now, but most often it was just Cloud Run, Artifact Registry, Cloud Build, and Google Cloud Storage. The rest of the services were basically known unknowns, and some services were even unknown unknowns...I literally stumbled upon them when answering prep exam questions. My honest reaction was always: "Are these services real?"

Most of my understanding of GCP came from the [Professional Cloud Architect path](https://www.skills.google/paths/12) on Google Cloud Skills Boost. The path and its various courses opened me up to the world of GCP from first principles. This was how I got to really understand the inner workings of GCP's VPC and other important services.

However, I felt Google Cloud Skills Boost alone wasn't enough, as I naturally gravitate towards reading when learning new things. I then used the [**Google Cloud Certified Professional Cloud Architect Study Guide**](https://www.amazon.com/Google-Cloud-Certified-Professional-Architect/dp/1119871050) by Dan Sullivan. This book gave me a better understanding of GCP services from an architect's perspective. I'd admit most of the info was largely geared toward passing the exam, but I really got to understand and borrow the mind of an architect. It even helped me provision the complete infrastructure for my final year project work using Terraform. That was time well spent.

Since the PCA exam was sponsored by Google through the GEAR program, I had access to top trainers who took us through the PCA exam training once a week for 6 weeks. I must say the explanations from Mark and Julien really drove home most of the important things I missed and struggled with, like Kubernetes and GKE.

This was the most difficult thing I had to understand in the whole course. I literally spent about a week just trying to understand Kubernetes and GKE, only to rewatch Mark's recorded training session the night before my exam on how containerization started from Google's own needs in 2004, to the birth of Docker, and then Kubernetes. This rewired my brain on how I understood Kubernetes, containerization, and microservices.
Mark from Jellyfish Training, this is an appreciation post. Thanks a lot.

## What Actually Helped Me Pass

Looking back, the practice tests found in the module files were quite similar to the actual exam. The key difference is that the exam is heavily focused on real-world scenarios.

**My Study Resources Breakdown:**
- **Weekly Modules & Recordings:** I reviewed the Weekly Module Files multiple times and rewatched the Recorded Sessions *(Note: These are in a private Google Drive from the GEAR program. If you'd like access, feel free to shoot me an email)*
- **Study Guide:** The [Google Cloud Certified Professional Cloud Architect Study Guide (2019)](https://www.amazon.com/Google-Cloud-Certified-Professional-Architect/dp/1119871050) by Dan Sullivan. Although it's an older book, it helped significantly
- **Practice Tests:** I focused on the tests provided in the Sullivan book and all practice exams included in the module files, especially the pre-exam quiz in Week_6_Preparing_for_PCA_Module_5_6.pdf
- **Technical Focus:** Study the Well-Architected Framework, Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform, and GKE. I encountered many questions regarding these topics

**About the Case Studies:**

A significant portion of the exam (about 14 out of 50 questions) is based on case studies. I specifically received the Altostrat Media and KnightMotives Automotive case studies. You don't need to memorize these, as they're provided in a side column during the exam, but being comfortable with them is crucial.

My preparation was quite difficult as I had to simultaneously juggle studying for missed quizzes and final year exams (I was in exams week), preparing for the PCA exam, working with Insurerity, and working on my final year project. This was quite challenging and stressful. I couldn't have a full sleep schedule for almost the whole of June.

I almost always feared that I'd fail the exam and often criticized myself for not taking the ACE exam instead. The sheer amount of information I needed to know and load into my brain was exhausting, coupled with other pressing responsibilities I had to attend to.

On **July 9, 2026 @ 11:00 AM**, I sat behind my PC for 120 minutes. At 1:17 PM, I checked my email and there was an email from Credly that said: **"You just earned a badge from Google Cloud."** 

Upon scrolling down, it revealed: **Professional Cloud Architect certification. Issuer: Google Cloud.**

In retrospect, I believe the challenge was worth it. The stress, frustration, and everything associated with this experience was worth it. It shows that I can achieve and grow in any circumstance, regardless of the odds, provided I put my trust in Allah and put in the hours and effort. Because in the end, every experience shapes us, and if you don't try, you'll never know.
